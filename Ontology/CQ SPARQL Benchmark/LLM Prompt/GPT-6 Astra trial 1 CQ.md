# Competency Questions for a Rice Pest and Disease Multimodal Knowledge Graph

The proposed multimodal knowledge graph (MMKG) will support evidence-based diagnosis, surveillance, and management research for pests and diseases of rice (*Oryza sativa*) by linking scientific and extension texts, plant images, environmental observations, and genomic or agronomic records. Its initial geographic focus is tropical lowland rice in Indonesia, with Southeast Asian evidence retained alongside its geographic and experimental applicability. The following competency questions (CQs) specify the entities, relationships, integration requirements, and queryable capabilities needed by plant pathology researchers, agricultural extension officers, and precision-agriculture developers.

## Assumptions and interpretation

- **Defaults adopted:** 40 CQs; the user groups and geographic focus stated above; common major rice pests and diseases; and no supplied multimodal dataset inventory, established MMKG schema, or prior MMKG CQ list. This is a proposed requirements specification, not a claim that the required data have already been acquired or integrated.
- **Domain coverage:** rice blast, bacterial leaf blight, sheath blight, tungro, brown planthopper, yellow stem borer, and rice bug, with brown spot included as a diagnostic alternative and visibly symptom-free plants as image baselines. Rice blast is represented using the source name *Magnaporthe oryzae*, with authority-supported synonyms retained; bacterial leaf blight retains the pathovar distinction in *Xanthomonas oryzae* pv. *oryzae* (Xoo). The disease examples are grounded in [IRRI's rice disease diagnostic guide](https://www.knowledgebank.irri.org/images/docs/diagnostic-of-common-diseases-of-rice.pdf).
- **Germplasm examples:** IR64, IR36, and Ciherang serve as cultivar search terms; IR24, IRBB5, and IRBB21 serve as experimental reference materials. Naming a cultivar does not assert a particular resistance allele or resistance phenotype. In particular, the distinction between an allele call and an isolate-specific resistance outcome is essential, as illustrated by [experimental comparisons of bacterial-blight resistance lines](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6098014/).
- **Modality definitions:** T = domain assertions derived from prose; I = visual observations, region annotations, and image-derived measurements; S = sensor or environmental measurements and their time-series derivatives; G = structured germplasm, genotype, assay, agronomic, and yield records. Identifiers, timestamps, units, provenance, and acquisition metadata accompany every modality and do not by themselves create a second modality. Converting image features or sensor readings into a table does not turn them into independent G evidence.
- **Query parameters:** “selected,” “specified,” and the reference time *t₀* denote parameters supplied when a query runs. Numerical filters below are illustrative evaluation settings, not established biological thresholds or management recommendations. Source-derived disease rules must retain their original conditions, applicability, and version.
- **Evidence and uncertainty:** visual symptoms, a reported diagnosis, a laboratory-supported diagnosis, a genotype call, and an experimentally observed resistance phenotype are separate assertions. A visually symptom-free image does not establish freedom from infection. Missing data, explicitly observed absence, and evidence of incompatibility must be distinguishable.
- **Computation:** image segmentation, feature extraction, rule compilation, and predictive-model fitting occur through documented upstream procedures; the graph stores their outputs and provenance for querying. CQs that compare or rank results require compatible measurement definitions and do not imply causal effects. No diagnostic probability is inferred merely by counting matching symptoms.

The distribution is **7 text CQs (17.5%), 7 image CQs (17.5%), 5 environmental CQs (12.5%), 5 genomic/tabular CQs (12.5%), and 16 fusion CQs (40%)**. Each category includes Simple, Relational, and Complex-Inferential questions. “Simple” means a direct fact or fixed-case evidence lookup; “Relational” requires traversal among domain entities; “Complex-Inferential” requires aggregation, comparison, temporal processing, or application of an explicit rule or stored model.

### Query and alignment conventions

Every answer should expose the relevant entity identifiers and evidence records. Instance-level fusion requires a documented link through the same plant, sample, plot, trial, or case and a compatible time interval; sharing only a disease name is insufficient to assert that two records describe the same event. Literature may instead join through an explicitly mapped symptom, pathogen, allele, or applicability condition.

Sensor durations use quality-passed observations and documented validity intervals, with missing intervals excluded rather than silently interpolated. Observation coverage is valid duration divided by expected duration under the acquisition schedule; units and time zones must be normalized. Comparative trials must retain isolate or insect-population identity, plant stage, measurement method, scale, management treatment, and sampling unit where relevant.

The class and relation names below are **proposed local schema terms**, not claims of existing ontology identifiers. The following are candidate alignments requiring term-level review:

| Requirement | Proposed alignment and affected CQs |

Preserve organism identity separately from the name of a disease or damage syndrome. |
| Plant traits, organs, visual qualities, and phenology | Review Plant Ontology for organs, [Plant Trait Ontology](https://obofoundry.org/ontology/to.html) for traits, and PATO for qualities, especially CQ-IMG-01–07 and CQ-GEN-02–05. **Terminology correction:** PPO is the [Plant Phenology Ontology](https://obofoundry.org/ontology/ppo.html), so its appropriate role is phenological observations rather than a general plant disease phenotype vocabulary. |

### Verification and data-readiness notes

The questions follow the requirements-oriented principle of using answerable questions to guide ontology design and evaluation, as set out by [Grüninger and Fox (1995), *Methodology for the Design and Evaluation of Ontologies*](https://www.eil.utoronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/enterprise-modelling/papers/gruninger-ijcai95.pdf). Formalization should give each CQ an input binding, a SPARQL or equivalent graph query, a declared entailment regime, an expected answer schema, and a small evidence-backed reference dataset. The present deliverable defines those requirements; it does not include an implemented ontology, executable queries, or measured query results.

For fusion verification, removing one required modality must remove the corresponding evidence support or make the answer incomplete; a precomputed score is insufficient unless its inputs remain traceable to every required modality. An empty result is acceptable when a complete queryable cohort contains no matches, but unavailable inputs should produce an explicit insufficient-data status. Source-derived rules must define the treatment of failed, missing, and explicitly absent criteria; contradictory observations remain available for review.

## 7. Scope boundary note

In scope are rice pest and disease terminology, visible symptom and damage observations, environmental exposure, accession-level genotype and resistance evidence, and agronomic outcomes needed for diagnosis support, surveillance, and research-oriented cultivar selection. The MMKG also represents evidence provenance, uncertainty, temporal and spatial alignment, and the conditions under which observations or claims can be compared. Out of scope are definitive diagnosis from images or weather alone, de novo gene-function discovery, unqualified causal attribution, pesticide-dose prescription, and autonomous implementation of management actions. CQ-IMG-06–07 and CQ-MM-05–08, CQ-MM-11–14, and CQ-MM-16 are conditional on annotated and linked field or trial records that were not supplied, with the dependencies above defining the required data audit. CQ-MM-15 additionally requires independently verified outcomes and stored model evaluations, so it remains a conditional evaluation requirement rather than an asserted predictive capability.
