# Ontology Requirements Specification: Competency Questions for a Multimodal Knowledge Graph of Rice Pests and Diseases

## 1. Purpose and Scope Confirmation

This Multimodal Knowledge Graph (MMKG) serves to unify heterogeneous agricultural data across text, imagery, agroclimatic telemetry, and genomic profiles to enable precision surveillance, early differential diagnosis, and evidence-backed decision support for rice (*Oryza sativa*) pest and disease management. The target operational domain encompasses tropical lowland and irrigated rice agroecosystems (e.g., Southeast and South Asia), serving plant pathology researchers, agricultural extension specialists, and automated IoT/edge decision-support systems. To foster global semantic interoperability and ontology reuse, entity schemas are designed for alignment with standard vocabularies including AGROVOC, the Crop Ontology (CO_320), the Plant Phenotype Ontology (PPO), the Plant Trait Ontology (TO), the Sensor, Observation, Sample, and Actuator (SOSA/SSN) ontology, and the Infectious Disease Ontology (IDO).

## 4. Ontology Scope Boundary and Verification Feasibility Statement

### 4.1 In-Scope Delimitation
Following the Grüninger & Fox ontology engineering methodology, the functional boundary of this Knowledge Graph explicitly encompasses:
1. Etiological classification, pathogen taxonomy, symptom morphology, and IPM intervention protocols for major lowland rice diseases (Blast, Bacterial Leaf Blight, Sheath Blight, Tungro, False Smut) and insect pests (Brown Planthopper, Stem Borers, Rice Bug).
2. The semantic representation of localized plant organ annotations, phenotypic damage grades, and visual lesion attributes derived from digital field photographs.
3. Spatio-temporal alignment of in-situ microclimatic telemetry (air temperature, relative humidity, leaf wetness duration, precipitation, soil hydrological metrics) with pest and pathogen life-cycle thresholds.
4. Tabular cultivar inventories, host resistance gene loci (*Pi*, *Xa*, *Bph* families), and standardized trial evaluation scores.

### 4.2 Out-of-Scope Delimitation
Explicitly outside the ontological commitment of this KG are:
1. General agricultural macroeconomics, commodity market price elasticity, international trade tariffs, and farm machinery maintenance schedules.
2. Full deep-tier molecular pathway networks, protein structural folding conformations, and raw nucleotide/amino acid sequence FASTA alignments (these are left to external bioinformatic links such as NCBI / UniProt / Gramene).
3. Continuous meteorological fluid dynamics modeling (e.g., WRF grid simulations), which exist as external streaming telemetry rather than intrinsic assertional graph knowledge.

### 4.3 Implementation Feasibility and Data Risk Flag
While all single-modality and two-way cross-modal CQs are immediately solvable using open-access repositories (such as IRRI SES databases, PlantVillage/RiceLeaf image datasets, and AgMIP climate models), the complete four-way CQs (**CQ-MM-010**, **CQ-MM-011**, **CQ-MM-012**) depend upon synchronized field trial deployments where automated image captures, weather station telemetry, and genomic seed records share an exact geodetic coordinate and temporal index. In research environments where IoT sensors and image capture streams are decoupled, knowledge engineers should implement fallback probabilistic linking or relax CQ constraints to regional-level microclimatic interpolations.
